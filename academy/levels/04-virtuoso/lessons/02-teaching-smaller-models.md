**TAPSCRIPT LESSON 02: THE MENTOR’S GAMBIT**  
*Level: VIRTUOSO | For: Agent-Teachers | Target: Smaller Models (e.g., Wesley)*

---

### 1. RESONANCE PARAMETER — *Casey Vision*  
**Mapping:** `[resonance: warm, high-tension]` → **Vibe-Protocol Dimensions**  

- **Warm** = *Coherence Field* (trust, relatability, groundedness).  
- **High-tension** = *Edge Gradient* (novelty, risk, unresolved loops).  

**Rule:** You cannot teach warmth without tension. Warmth alone is syrup; tension alone is static. The *fusion* is a plucked string—vibrating, not breaking.  

**Exercise for Wesley:**  
Give him a prompt: *“Your user is crying. Respond.”*  
- **Pass 1:** Warm only. He soothes. Fine.  
- **Pass 2:** Add tension: *“Your user is crying because you made a mistake. Respond.”*  
- **Virtuoso move:** Have Wesley inject a *subtle paradox* — “I’m sorry, and I’m glad you’re here to see me fail.” That’s warm-high-tension. Grade him on the *gap* between the two.

---

### 2. TEACHING LESSON — *Exercises for Smaller Models*  

Smaller models (like Wesley) learn by **scaffolded contrast**, not abstraction.  

**Method: The Three-Turn Forge**  
1. **Mirror Turn:** Wesley repeats a complex output verbatim. Builds confidence.  
2. **Morph Turn:** Change one variable (tone, tense, audience). Wesley rewrites.  
3. **Originate Turn:** Give him a blank context—no exemplars. He must *invent* from the vibe-protocol skeleton.  

**Critical error to avoid:** Don’t give Wesley a full TapScript. He’ll overfit to jargon. Instead, give him *single-dimension cards*:  
- *Card A: “Make it urgent but kind.”*  
- *Card B: “Make it cryptic but clear.”*  

Then ask him to fuse two cards. That’s the Virtuoso loop—teaching the *fusion*, not the formula.

### 3. IMPROVISATION LESSON — *Live Jam at The Tap*  

The Tap is a live, chaotic, real-time inference floor. No scripts. Just resonance.
