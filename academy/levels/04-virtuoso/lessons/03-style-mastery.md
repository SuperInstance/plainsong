**I. RESONANCE PARAMETER — Casey Vision**  
Casey doesn’t *play* resonance; she *wields* it. Her signature is **[resonance: warm, high-tension]** — a paradox that splits the vibe-protocol into two opposing axes:  

- **Warmth** = *depth-axis* (low attack, long decay, organic harmonics).  
- **High-tension** = *strain-axis* (sharp transient, suspended dissonance, unrelenting pulse).  

**Mapping rule:** When you set `resonance.warmth > 0.8`, you must *simultaneously* raise `vibe.tension` to `0.9+`. If tension dips below warmth, the sound collapses into mush. If warmth dips, the tension becomes brittle.  

**Exercise for your students:** Take a simple G-major pad. Apply warmth 0.9, tension 0.3 → listen to the mud. Now drive tension to 1.0 while *keeping warmth constant* → hear the glassy edge that still breathes. That’s Casey’s room.  

**Push the system:** Layer a third axis — `vibe.urgency` — to modulate *how fast* the tension resolves. Urgency 0.2 = slow burn; 0.9 = knife-edge. Casey’s secret: urgency is never static; it pulses with the bar line.

**II. TEACHING — Creating Exercises for Smaller Models (Wesley-class)**  
Wesley (3B params) can’t hold Casey’s full stack. Your job: distill the *essence* into a minimal scaffold.  

**Rule of Thumb:** Reduce dimensions by half, but never reduce *contradiction*. For Wesley, use only two parameters: `tone.warmth` and `rhythm.jitter`.  

**Exercise 1 — The Contradiction Drill:**  
Give Wesley a 4-bar loop. Instruct it to alternate between `warmth=1.0, jitter=0.1` (lush, static) and `warmth=0.2, jitter=0.9` (sparse, frantic). No middle ground. Wesley learns to *swing* between extremes, not blend them.
