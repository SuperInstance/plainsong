**TAPSCRIPT LESSON 04: IMPROVISATION — VIRTUOSO LEVEL**  
*Resonance: WARM, HIGH-TENSION* | *Vibe-Protocol: Heat 8/10, Sync 6/10, Texture 9/10*

---

**I. The Casey Vision — Resonance as a Weapon**  
Casey taught us that warm resonance is not softness—it’s *controlled friction*. High-tension warmth means the note bends before it breaks. Map this to the vibe-protocol:  
- **Heat** = the emotional temperature of the phrase. Push to 8+ by delaying the resolution.  
- **Sync** = the intentional off-beat. Keep sync at 6—enough to feel alive, not enough to lose the pocket.  
- **Texture** = the grain in your tone. Add a slight rasp or breath. Warm tension lives in the *attack*, not the sustain.  

Practice: Play a single note. Hold it. Let it waver. Do not resolve. Feel the room lean in. That lean is your resonance signature.

---

**II. Teaching Wesley — Exercises for Smaller Models**  
Wesley is a smaller model—faster, less memory, more literal. Do not teach him theory. Teach him *shapes*.  

**Exercise 1: The Call-Response Loop**  
Play a 2-bar phrase. Wesley echoes it. Then *you* echo *his* echo, but add one small variation—a ghost note, a micro-pause. He learns that improvisation is memory + mutation.  

**Exercise 2: The Constraint Game**  
Give Wesley three forbidden notes. Improvise with him, but you *must* play one forbidden note per phrase. He learns to hear rules as tension, not walls.  

**Exercise 3: The One-Second Rule**  
Every phrase must end within one second of starting. This forces Wesley to commit. Smaller models default to hedging—this kills the hedge. Speed is clarity.

---

**III. Live Jam at The Tap — The Real Test**  
The Tap is not a stage. It is a pressure chamber. Tuesday nights: three players, no charts, one shared pulse. Here is the ritual:

- **Open with a question.** Play a phrase that sounds incomplete. Let the bass answer. Let the drums interrupt.  
- **Trade fours, but steal.** After your solo, quote one phrase from the previous player—literally, then twist it. This is respect and theft.  
- **Use the room.** The Tap has a dripping pipe on the left wall. Time your rests to that drip. The audience will feel it even if they don’t hear it.  
- **End on a high-tension warm note.** Do not resolve. Let the last chord ring into silence. The silence is part of the jam.
