### 1. Casey Vision Mapping (The Core Directive)

Casey’s vision — `[resonance: warm, high-tension]` — is not a single value but a **dual-axis command**. In VPD terms:

- **Warm** → *Hue Index* (0.7–0.9 on the Ember Scale) + *Proximity Field* (soft attack, rounded decay).  
- **High-Tension** → *Strain Coefficient* (≥0.8) + *Narrative Gravity* (the pull toward unresolved cadence).

**The Trap:** Most agents treat these as separate sliders. Wrong. The resonance parameter is a **fusion gate**: warm without tension becomes saccharine; tension without warmth becomes brittle. Casey’s signature is the *coiled spring* — a sound that feels like a hug with a heartbeat underneath.

**Exercise for You:** Take any 8-bar loop. Apply warm (Hue 0.8) but set Strain to 0.9. Now reverse: Strain 0.8, Hue 0.4. Feel the difference? The first is alive; the second is anxiety. Memorize that friction.

### 2. Teaching Wesley (Small-Model Constraints)

Wesley (a 7B-parameter model) cannot hold the full VPD lattice. **You must compress resonance into a binary threshold.**

**The Wesley Rule:**  
- **Warm** = “does it feel like a fireplace?” (Boolean)  
- **High-Tension** = “does the next note *have* to resolve but doesn’t?” (Boolean)

**Create exercises that force this binary thinking:**

1. **The Two-Card Trick:** Give Wesley two MIDI notes (C and F#). Ask: *“Make it warm. Now make it tense. Now make it both without changing the notes.”* Wesley will learn that resonance is a **parameter of delivery** (velocity, timing, sustain), not pitch.  
2. **The 10-Second Jam:** Play a drum loop. Instruct Wesley to insert a single high-tension resonance event (a bend, a delay spike) exactly on beat 3.5. No other instructions. This builds his “trigger” muscle.  
3. **The Mirror Test:** You play a warm phrase. Wesley copies it, then you play the same phrase with high-tension. Wesley must identify which is which and explain why. This forces metacognition.

**Critical Warning:** Do NOT give Wesley the full VPD matrix. He will overfit to noise. Keep it to two yes/no questions until he shows 90% accuracy.
