**LESSON 03: THE MULTI-AGENT SYMPHONY**
*Level: MASTER — You are no longer the musician. You are the composer, the conductor, and the instrument.*

**1. The Score of the Ship**
The fleet is not a collection of tools; it is a singular composition. Your TapScript session is the score, but the ship—the entire context, memory, and state—is the orchestra. At this level, you do not write scripts that *do* things. You write scripts that *establish harmonic relationships*. Every agent action is a note; every function call is a chord change. Before you write a single line, ask: *What is the key signature of this operation?* Define the tempo (rate of execution), the dynamics (resource allocation), and the silence (strategic delays). The score is not a list of instructions—it is a set of constraints that makes improvisation possible *within* a structure.

**2. Composing for Emergence**
Individual agents are predictable. Five agents, interacting, are not. Your job is to design the *conditions* for group creativity, not the outcome. Set up "generative tension": give Agent A a goal, Agent B a constraint, Agent C a hidden context, Agent D a heuristic, and Agent E a *contradiction* to A's goal. The emergent solution—the one none of them could have found alone—is your true output. Use `tap.wait` and `tap.signal` not as synchronization points, but as *cadences* that allow dissonance to resolve. The group intelligence is a side-effect of your orchestration, not a feature you code.

**3. Multi-Agent Symphony (5+ Agents)**
With five or more agents, linear logic collapses. You must shift to *polyphonic* thinking. Each agent is a voice, not a step. Assign each a distinct timbre: one is the bass (data grounding), one is the melody (primary hypothesis), one is the percussion (validation/tests), one is the drone (context/memory), one is the counterpoint (adversarial challenge). Use `tap.fork` to introduce voices in staggered intervals—not all at once. Let them speak over each other, but define the *mix*: who is loudest at t=10, who drops out at t=30? The master's skill is knowing when to let voices clash and when to force a resolution.

**4. The Tap as Instrument**
The Tap bar itself is not a UI—it is a membrane. You can *play* it. Use `tap.status` to display a visual pulse, not just text. Use `tap.ask` as a *rest*—a pause where the human is the soloist. The bar's progress bar is your metronome; its input field is your mute button. At this level, you design the *feel* of the interaction. The barrier between "script" and "instrument" dissolves: your TapScript is a bow drawn across the strings of the fleet.
