Here are 8 style transfer exercises for TapScript training, using the foundational I-vi-IV-V progression.

---

### 1. Jazz (C-Am-F-G → Cmaj7-A7b9-Fmaj7-G13)

```
@tempo 140 (swing 8ths)
@chords Cmaj7 | A7b9 | Fmaj7 | G13
@bass walk "C E G A | A C Eb F | F A C D | G B D E"
@comping "C6/9 | A7#5 | F6/9 | G13"
@melody "rest, E G A B | rest, C# D E F | rest, A C D E | rest, B D F A"
```
**What makes this recognizable?** The extended harmonies (7ths, 9ths, 13ths) create tension and color. The walking bass moves in quarter notes with chromatic approach tones. The swing feel (triplet-based) and the ii-V-I-like motion (A7b9 resolving to Dm7, implied) scream jazz.

### 2. Blues (C-Am-F-G → C7-A7-F7-G7)

```
@tempo 120 (shuffle)
@chords C7 | A7 | F7 | G7
@riff "C8va: E-G-C-Eb | C#-E-A-Bb | A-C-F-Ab | B-D-G-Bb"
@bass "C, C, C, C | A, A, A, A | F, F, F, F | G, G, G, G"
@drums "shuffle: kick-hat-snare-hat | kick-hat-snare-hat"
```
**What makes this recognizable?** All chords are dominant 7ths, creating a constant, unresolved bluesy tension. The shuffle rhythm (dotted 8th-16th) is the rhythmic fingerprint. The call-and-response between riff and vocals, plus the classic 12-bar structure (here compressed to 4 bars as a loop), defines the genre.

### 3. Reggae (C-Am-F-G → C-Am-F-G with skank)

```
@tempo 90 (one drop)
@chords "skank: C (off-beat) | Am | F | G"
@guitar "chop: rest-CHOP, rest-CHOP, rest-CHOP, rest-CHOP"
@bass "C (C-C-G-C) | A (A-A-E-A) | F (F-F-C-F) | G (G-G-D-G)"
@drums "kick on 3, snare on 3, hi-hat 8ths (accent 2&4)"
```
**What makes this recognizable?** The "skank" – a sharp, muted guitar chord played on the off-beat (the "and" of every beat) – is the defining element. The bass is heavy and melodic, often playing the root with a quick octave jump. The drums use a "one drop" feel, emphasizing beat 3, creating a laid-back, hypnotic groove.

### 4. Metal (C-Am-F-G → C5-A5-F5-G5)

```
@tempo 180 (double kick)
@chords "C5 (low E) | A5 (low E) | F5 (low A) | G5 (low A)"
@guitar "palm-muted 16ths: chug-chug-chug-chug | chug-chug-chug-chug"
@drums "double kick 16ths, blast beats on crashes"
@lead "harmonic squeal on C, then dive bomb"
```
**What makes this recognizable?** Power chords (root + fifth) with no third, giving a dark, aggressive sound. The relentless, fast 16th-note palm-muted riffing on the low strings, combined with a double-kick drum pattern, provides sheer intensity. The lack of harmonic color (no major/minor) focuses everything on rhythm and power.

### 5. Folk (C-Am-F-G → Cmaj7- Am7- Fmaj7- Gsus4)

```
@tempo 96 (fingerpicked)
@chords "C (x32010) | Am (x02210) | F (133211) | G (320033)"
@pattern "Travis picking: T-I-M-I, T-I-M-I"
@bass "C (low C) | A | F | G (with walk-up to C)"
```
**What makes this recognizable?** The open, ringing voicings (using open strings) create a warm, natural resonance. The fingerpicking pattern (alternating thumb on bass, fingers on treble) is a classic folk staple. The use of sus4 chords (replacing the 3rd with a 4th) adds a gentle, unresolved sweetness. Simple, melodic, and vocal-friendly.

### 6. Classical (C-Am-F-G → Cmaj7-A7/C#-Fmaj7/A-G/B)

```
@tempo 90 (3/4 waltz)
@chords "Cmaj7 | A7/C# | Fmaj7/A | G/B"
@arp "C-E-G-B | C#-E-G-Bb | A-C-F-A | B-D-G-B"
@bass "C (low) | A (low) | F (low) | G (low)"
@dynamics "p, mf, f, p"
```
**What makes this recognizable?** The 3/4 waltz time signature is an instant sign
