# TapScript Progression Pattern Library

## Agent Training Module: Harmonic DNA

---

### 1. I-V-vi-IV (The Pop-Punk Axis)
**TapScript:**
```
BAR 1: C  G  Am  F  || BAR 2: C  G  Am  F
```
**Feeling:** Nostalgic optimism with a floor-pump kick—the emotional equivalent of a coming-of-age montage.

**Songs:** "Don't Stop Believin'" (Journey), "With or Without You" (U2), "Let It Be" (Beatles).

**Use when:** You need instant singalong accessibility. Works for verses, choruses, or whole songs that demand zero listener friction.

**Transformation:** Change the IV (F) to a iv (Fm). The brightness collapses into melancholy—suddenly the optimism feels fragile, as if the narrator is lying to themselves. This is the "Radiohead trick."

---

### 2. ii-V-I (The Jazz Engine)
**TapScript:**
```
BAR 1: Dm7  G7  || BAR 2: Cmaj7  (hold)
```
**Feeling:** Inevitable resolution—a controlled descent into home base, like a plane landing perfectly.

**Songs:** "Autumn Leaves," "Fly Me to the Moon," "All the Things You Are."

**Use when:** You want sophisticated forward motion. This is the default for jazz standards, bossa nova, and any progression requiring harmonic gravity.

**Transformation:** Replace the V (G7) with a tritone substitution (Db7). The resolution becomes darker, unexpected, and sneakier. The landing still happens but you arrive sideways.

---

### 3. I-IV-V (The Blues Skeleton)
**TapScript:**
```
BAR 1: C  F  || BAR 2: G  C
```
**Feeling:** Raw, declarative, and physically grounded—the musical equivalent of a firm handshake.

**Songs:** "Louie Louie" (Kingsmen), "Wild Thing" (Troggs), countless garage-rock staples.

**Use when:** You need maximum impact with minimum harmonic complexity. Perfect for punk, boogie-woogie, and folk singalongs.

**Transformation:** Add a bVII (Bb) between the IV and V. The primitive two-chord feel becomes bluesier, and the gravity shifts from major-key brightness to a modal, pentatonic-friendly grit.

---

### 4. Am-F-C-G (The Sad-Sweet Loop)
**TapScript:**
```
BAR 1: Am  F  || BAR 2: C  G
```
**Feeling:** Wistful introspection with a walking-forward pulse—like staring out a rain-streaked window.

**Songs:** "Zombie" (Cranberries), "California Dreamin'" (Mamas & Papas), "Someone Like You" (Adele, transposed).

**Use when:** You want minor-key emotion without full darkness. This progression keeps the relative major (C) as a safety net.

**Transformation:** Change the final G to an E major (the V of Am). Now you've created a dominant pull back to Am, making the loop tighter and more restless. The wistfulness becomes urgent longing.

---

### 5. i-VII-VI-V (The Andalusian Cousin)
**TapScript:**
```
BAR 1: Am  G  || BAR 2: F  E
```
**Feeling:** Dramatic, descending, theatrical—like walking down a grand staircase in a cape.

**Songs:** "Hit the Road Jack" (Ray Charles), "Smooth Criminal" (Michael Jackson), "Walk" (Pantera, metal version).

**Use when:** You want baroque drama or flamenco-adjacent tension. Works well for builds and pre-choruses.

**Transformation:** Change the final E to an E7. The dominant seventh adds a jazzier, more sinister edge. The drama shifts from theatrical to film-noir criminal.

---

### 6. I-vi-IV-V (The Doo-Wop Dream)
**TapScript:**
```
BAR 1: C  Am  || BAR 2: F  G
```
**Feeling:** Innocent, romantic, and slightly dated—like a 1950s soda fountain with a jukebox.

**Songs:** "Stand by Me" (Ben E. King), "Earth Angel" (Penguins), "Unchained Melody" (Righteous Brothers).

**Use when:** You want timeless romantic sentiment. Excellent for ballads and wedding-song-adjacent material.

**Transformation:** Swap the I (C) for a III (E). Now you get C's relative minor pull immediately—the progression becomes unexpectedly modal and modern, shedding the 1950s innocence for indie-folk complexity.

---

### 7. vi-IV-I-V (The Canon Variant)
**TapScript:**
```
BAR 1: Am  F  || BAR 2: C  G
```
**Feeling:** Emotionally open and anthemic—the "hands-in-the-air" progression.

**Songs:** "What's My Age Again?" (Blink-182), "Don't Stop Believin'" (chorus), "Africa" (Toto, verses).

**Use when:** You want the pop-punk axis but starting from the emotional low point. Great for choruses that need to feel like a release.

**Transformation:** Change the V (G) to a III (E). The anthemic release becomes a cliffhanger—instead of resolving, you hang on a dominant that wants to go home but never does.

---

### 8. I-III-IV-vi (The Bright-False-Sad)
**TapScript:**
```
BAR 1: C  E  || BAR 2: F  Am
```
**Feeling:** Chromatic lift followed by a warm fall—like a fairground ride with a surprise dip.

**Songs:** "Creep" (Radiohead, pre-chorus), "Space Oddity" (Bowie, bridge), "Sweet Child O' Mine" (Guns N' Roses, intro).

**Use when:** You want a sudden color change that feels both foreign and familiar. The III chord (E) is borrowed from the parallel minor.

**Transformation:** Change the III (E) to a III7 (E7). The borrowed chord becomes a secondary dominant, pulling hard toward the IV (F). The naive brightness becomes jazzy and sophisticated.
