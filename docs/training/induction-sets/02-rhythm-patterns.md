Here is a rhythm induction training set for AI agents learning TapScript. The agent should read the pattern, simulate the timing mentally (or via a click track), and then identify the genre before revealing the answer.

---

**Agent Protocol:** For each example, parse the TapScript. Sustain dots (`.`) = held note, rest dashes (`-`) = silence, note names = pitch trigger. Compile the timing, feel the pulse, and state your genre hypothesis.

---

**Example 1:**
`Kick: . - . - . - . - | . - . - . - . -`
`Snare: - - - - . - - - | - - - - . - - -`
`Hat: . . . . . . . . | . . . . . . . .` (played as straight 16ths, but with a lazy swing on the offbeats)

**What genre is this?**
**Answer:** *Boom-Bap Hip Hop.* The kick lands on beats 1 and 3, the snare cracks on beat 3 (backbeat), but the hats are the tell: they’re written as even 16ths, yet the human feel drags the second 16th of each beat slightly late. This “head-nod” swing, with the kick and snare forming a sparse, heavy pocket, is the DNA of 90s boom-bap. The rests between kicks create space; the groove breathes.

**Example 2:**
`Guitar (skank): . - . - . - . - | . - . - . - . -`
`Bass: . . . . . . . . | . . . . . . . .` (eighth notes, but the bass skips beat 1 often)
`Drums: - . - . - . - . | - . - . - . - .` (snare on beats 2 and 4, but rimshot, not crack)

**What genre is this?**
**Answer:** *Reggae Skank.* The guitar plays a short, choked “chop” on the offbeats (the “and” of each beat), not on the downbeats. The empty downbeats create the signature bounce. The bass is busy and melodic, playing a syncopated counter-melody, while the drums only hint at a backbeat. The rhythmic signature is *upbeat stabs, heavy bass displacement, and a relaxed, quarter-note pulse that feels like it’s leaning backward.*

**Example 3:**
`Bass: . - . . - . | . - . . - .`
`Chord: . - - . - - | . - - . - -` (one hit per measure)
`Melody: . . . . . . | . . . . . .` (flowing, but grouped in 3s)

**What genre is this?**
**Answer:** *Waltz in 3/4.* The bass hits on beat 1, then skips beat 2, then hits again on beat 3. The chord only rings on beat 1 of each bar. The melody’s phrasing groups into three-beat units. The rhythmic signature is a *triple meter with a strong downbeat and a lighter, secondary pulse on beat 3* – the classic “oom-pah-pah” in a stylized, minimalist arrangement. No backbeat, no 4/4 squareness.
