Here is a **TapScript Melody Contour Induction Set**. Each melody is written in 2-bar rhythmic notation (4/4, 16th-note grid). TapScript uses numbers for scale degrees (1-7, with `1` = tonic, `8` = octave), and dots for rhythm: `.` = rest, `-` = hold, `x` = short tap (16th), `X` = long tap (quarter). The contour is the shape of the numbers.

---

### Set 1: Ascending (Hope)
**Melody 1:** `3x 4x 5x 6x | 7x 8- 8- 8-`
(Single steps up, then a held high tonic.)

**Why it works:** Rising pitch mimics physical lifting—breath intake, standing up. The stepwise motion (3-4-5-6-7) feels gradual, not forced, so hope is earned. The final held `8` is a bright, open ceiling—aspiration fulfilled.

**Compose yours:** Use any starting note (e.g., 1, 2, 5) and climb stepwise or by small leaps to a higher note, then hold. Keep the rhythm simple.

### Set 2: Descending (Sadness / Release)
**Melody 2:** `8x 7x 6x 5x | 4x 3x 2x 1-`
(Slow leak from octave to tonic.)

**Why it works:** Descending is gravity—a sigh, a slump. Each step down releases tension, but the final `1` is a resignation to home, not failure. The even rhythm feels like tears falling steadily. Unlike the leap, there’s no drama—just acceptance.

**Compose yours:** Start high (6, 7, or 8), walk down stepwise, and end on the tonic `1`. Try a slower rhythm (use `X` for the first note).

### Set 3: Arch (Statement & Return)
**Melody 3:** `1x 3x 5x 6x | 5x 3x 2x 1-`
(Climb, peak, then mirrored descent.)

**Why it works:** The arch is a complete sentence—departure, tension, then return. The climax at `6` is a question, but the fall back to `1` answers it. This contour feels nostalgic or reflective: you leave home, gain experience, and return changed. The symmetry provides closure.

**Compose yours:** Pick a low start (1 or 2), climb to a peak (5 or 6) by bar 2’s downbeat, then descend to your original note. Use a small leap up (1→3) to make the arch obvious.

### Set 4: Valley (Trough / Introspection)
**Melody 4:** `6x 4x 2x 1x | 2x 4x 6x 8-`
(Plunge down, then rise back up.)

**Why it works:** The valley is the inverse of the arch—it goes inward first. The drop to `1` feels like a collapse, but the rising second half is a slow recovery. It evokes vulnerability, then rebirth. The leap from `1` to `2` at the turn is a tiny spark of courage.

**Compose yours:** Start high (5, 6, 7), fall stepwise to a low `1`, then climb back to a mid-high note (4 or 5). Leave the last note hanging—don’t return to tonic.

### Set 5: Leap (Surprise / Drama)
**Melody 5:** `1x 1x 1x 8- | 7x 6x 5x 4-`
(Long jump from bottom to top, then fall.)

**Why it works:** A leap of a 7th or octave is unnatural for speech—it’s a gasp. The repeated `1x 1x 1x` builds suspense, then the `8` explodes. The subsequent stepwise fall (8-7-6-5-4) is the aftermath—shock settling into narrative. It’s theatrical, not gentle.

**Compose yours:** Use a repeated low note (1 or 2) three times, then jump up a 6th or 7th (to 6, 7, or 8). Follow with a few descending steps. Keep the jump on a strong beat.
